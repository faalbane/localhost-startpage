What excites me most about Perplexity is the challenge of making AI-powered search not just accurate, but genuinely trustworthy and useful at scale. Having spent the last decade building AI/ML pipelines across healthcare, enterprise SaaS, and content authentication, I have seen firsthand how the gap between "technically impressive" and "actually reliable" is where the hardest engineering problems live. Perplexity sits right at that intersection.

Specifically, I am drawn to the retrieval-augmented generation infrastructure and the systems that determine what sources to surface, how to synthesize conflicting information, and how to present answers with appropriate confidence levels. At CopyCatch.AI, I architected pipelines for content authentication that required similar tradeoffs between speed, accuracy, and explainability. Bringing that experience to search, where millions of users depend on the output being both fast and factually grounded, is the kind of high-stakes technical challenge I thrive on.

I am also excited about the product engineering side of things. Perplexity has done something rare in making a complex AI system feel simple and intuitive. As someone who has built products from zero to exit (including through Y Combinator), I understand how much deliberate engineering goes into making something feel effortless. I want to help push that further, whether it is improving answer quality, expanding into new modalities, or building the infrastructure that lets the team ship faster without sacrificing reliability.

The fact that Perplexity is competing with entrenched incumbents by being fundamentally better, not just different, resonates with how I have approached every company I have built.
